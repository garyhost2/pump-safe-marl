# The cost column: three candidate causes tested, and a premise corrected

`docs/STEMS_COMPARISON.md` section 4 reports that every one of the eight published cost
ratios in arXiv:2510.14112v1 Table I sits below what we measure for the same method, names
three candidate causes -- the aggregation, the normalising rule, the tariff -- and declines
to pick one. This note picks.

Everything here is regenerated by

    source scripts/env.sh
    python -m experiments.diagnostics.cost_column_hypotheses --stage dump \
        --results results/t1_repro_ep15 --out experiments/diagnostics/cost_column
    python -m experiments.diagnostics.cost_column_hypotheses --stage analyse \
        --out experiments/diagnostics/cost_column

The dump stage replays all 39 stored runs of `results/t1_repro_ep15` from their saved
models and asks `evaluate` for its accumulator, because the stored records carry aggregate
KPIs only and every hypothesis here needs the per-step series. **The replay is exact: the
annual cost of all 39 runs reproduces its stored value to 0.000000 USD.** The
`price-only-battery` arm has no stored record -- it is defined in the diagnostic script
itself and run fresh.

## Verdict in one paragraph

**None of the three explains the eight rows, and the question itself was posed on a premise
that does not hold.** The eight-for-eight offset is specific to the fifteen-episode budget.
At fifty episodes, with no change to the cost definition, the tariff, the aggregation or the
normaliser, three of the eight rows land on their published figures against *our own*
unshielded rule at 18,773 USD -- MARLISA 0.847 against published 0.847, D-MAPPO 0.817
against 0.803, MADCQ 0.833 against 0.814. Five rows are still more than 0.05 above their
published figure at fifty episodes -- MPC, Single-Agent SAC, MADDPG, MetaEMS and our own
arm -- but none of those five gaps is a cost-definition gap either: MPC's is bracketed by
its own forecaster, three are energy gaps, and the last is ours. Of the three hypotheses as
asked: the aggregation explains
nothing (no row moves by more than 0.008, against gaps of 0.067 to 0.694, and not even
consistently in sign); the tariff is excluded by proof rather than by survey (no
non-negative time-of-use tariff reproduces the eight published ratios on our hourly import
profiles); and the normalising rule, which looked like the survivor on the fifteen-episode
table, is **refuted by the fifty-episode runs** -- the baseline those rows implied falls
from about 22,000 USD to 18,782-19,200 USD once the policies are trained, i.e. onto the rule
we already have. What is left is not a cost definition at all. Three rows still disagree
because of energy rather than money -- at fifty episodes Single-Agent SAC, MADDPG and
MetaEMS buy 1.388, 0.996 and 1.147 times the rule's electricity where the paper reports
0.813, 0.856 and 0.835 -- and the eighth row is our own arm, 0.104 short at a matched
budget, which is a result about our method. Three reproduce, one is a forecaster, three are
an energy gap, one is ours.

**This note's own first draft got that wrong**, and the correction is recorded rather than
quietly removed: section 4 below sets out the five-row normalising-baseline story the
fifteen-episode table supports, and then refutes it with the fifty-episode runs. It is the
project's documented failure mode -- a narrative fitted to the rows that suggested it
-- caught here by a budget check that costs nothing because the runs were already on disk.

### Correction to a premise: this is not a 0.2783 USD/kWh Travis tariff

The brief for this work, and informal notes before it, call the flat reference "the Travis
mean, 0.2783 USD/kWh". Measured, the series the environment serves has a mean of
**0.27314** USD/kWh, and it is not a Travis tariff at all: `tx_travis_8b`'s schema carries
`"pricing": null` for every building, and the pricing actually billed is the
`citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all` file, which `stems/household.py` already documents as
Fontana, California. The 1.9 per cent difference changes no number in this note -- a
ratio column is invariant to a uniform rescaling of the tariff -- but the label is wrong
and should not propagate.

## 1. The premise: the eight-for-eight offset is a fifteen-episode artifact

Every hypothesis in this note is a hypothesis about the cost *definition*. A definition
cannot depend on how long a policy trained, so the first thing to check is whether a row's
gap survives a larger budget -- and it costs nothing, because the runs are on disk
(`results/t1_unshielded_ep50`, `results/t1_repro_ep50`). The normaliser is the same
unshielded rule at 18,773 USD: it does not learn, so it is the same number at both budgets.

| MetaEMS | 1.315 | 24,904 | 1.327 | 0.836 | +0.491 | 29,790 |

| MADCQ | 0.881 | 15,629 | **0.833** | 0.814 | **+0.019** | 19,200 |

| STEMS / ours | 1.011 | 16,825 | 0.896 | 0.792 | +0.104 | 21,243 |

Five seeds per learned row; MPC does not learn, so its row is the same rollout at both
budgets. **Three of eight reproduce against our own rule once trained**, with the real

The row that matters most for the rest of this note is the last column. If the implied

**MPC's remaining gap is the forecaster, not the cost.** Their paper specifies none. Running
our own MPC twice and changing only that (`results/t1_mpc_probe`): causal forecaster 18,374
USD / ratio 0.979, perfect foresight 14,448 USD / ratio 0.770, their published 0.872 sitting
between the two. The oracle row is an upper bound from information no controller has, never
a result -- but the bracket means MPC's cost row needs no cost-definition explanation
either.

That leaves **Single-Agent SAC, MADDPG and MetaEMS**, and their disagreement is about energy
rather than money: at fifty episodes they import 1.388, 0.996 and 1.147 times the rule's
electricity against the paper's 0.813, 0.856 and 0.835. The project has already diagnosed
those three independently -- saturated pre-squash actions on 86.6 to 97.4 per cent of
building-steps, and MetaEMS taking 3,200 gradient steps where the others take 437,950.

`stems_table_compare`'s docstring records a Spearman rho of 0.929 between a method's
intervention rate and its gap to the published cost, which reads as the shield inflating
cost. That is a correlation across methods; the within-method measurement is already on
disk, because the same seven comparison controllers were run at the same budget with

**One baseline does not explain all eight.** The implied values span 20,316 to 34,584 USD,

unshielded, it imports 87,109 kWh against the rule's 73,206 and costs 22,777 USD, and an
unshielded rule violating its state-of-charge bound 35.1 per cent of the time is plausibly in
that family.

### 4.1 Why it is wrong

An implied baseline is a property of the normaliser. It must not move when a policy trains
longer. It moves:

| row | implied baseline at ep15 | implied baseline at ep50 | our rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| MARLISA | 21,785 | **18,782** | 18,773 |
| D-MAPPO | 23,026 | **19,108** | 18,773 |
| MADCQ | 20,316 | **19,200** | 18,773 |
| MPC (does not learn) | 21,073 | 21,071 | 18,773 |
| STEMS / ours | 23,956 | 21,243 | 18,773 |
| MADDPG | 29,111 | 24,717 | 18,773 |
| MetaEMS | 29,524 | 29,790 | 18,773 |
| Single-Agent SAC | 34,584 | 35,840 | 18,773 |

Three of the five rows the 21,275-23,160 USD window "explained" converge on **our own rule**
once trained -- MARLISA to within 0.05 per cent of it. Fitting a 22,000 USD normaliser to
them was fitting their training budget. Worse, the fitted baseline is now actively wrong for
those rows: at 22,000 USD, MARLISA's fifty-episode cost of 15,908 USD reads **0.723** against
a published 0.847, a residual of -0.124 where our own rule gives +0.000.

Two rows do not converge: MetaEMS (29,524 -> 29,790) and Single-Agent SAC (34,584 ->
35,840), which is consistent with those two being broken rather than undertrained -- and a
hypothesis that survives only on the two rows the project has already diagnosed as broken is
not a hypothesis about the cost column.

**H2 is rejected.** The window is real arithmetic on the fifteen-episode table and is left
above exactly as it was computed, because the arithmetic is not what was wrong; the inference
from it was.

## 5. H3, the tariff: ruled out, and not by enumeration

Candidate tariffs, each applied to every arm and renormalised to the rule under the same
tariff. `mean abs residual` is averaged over the eight rows; `rows within 0.05` counts rows
reproduced at our own baseline.

| variant | baseline USD | mean abs residual | rows within 0.05 | implied-baseline CV | implied-baseline range USD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| actual five-level TOU | 18,773 | 0.290 | 0 | 0.187 | 20,316-34,584 |
| flat at the series mean | 19,995 | 0.219 | 0 | 0.143 | 21,708-32,196 |
| TOU flattened 75 per cent of the way | 19,690 | 0.236 | 0 | 0.153 | 21,549-32,793 |
| TOU flattened halfway | 19,384 | 0.253 | 0 | 0.164 | 21,391-33,390 |
| TOU sharpened 1.5x | 18,162 | 0.329 | 1 | 0.211 | 19,222-35,777 |
| TOU + exports credited at the tariff | 8,728 | 0.287 | 0 | 0.332 | 7,632-19,268 |
| TOU + district netting before billing | 17,602 | 0.219 | 3 | 0.211 | 16,817-30,602 |
| flat + exports credited | 8,908 | 0.142 | 4 | 0.204 | 8,234-14,755 |

Flattening the tariff moves the mean residual from 0.290 to 0.219 -- it removes about a
quarter of the average gap and reproduces no row to 0.05. Why it helps at all is structural
and worth stating, because it is also a check: **at a perfectly flat tariff the cost ratio
is the electricity-consumption ratio by construction**, and the measurement confirms it
(0.947, 1.327, 1.177, 1.140, 0.974, 0.916, 0.920, 0.933 -- identical to the consumption
ratios in section 0 to three decimals). So H3's entire reach is the distance between our
cost and consumption columns, which is up to 0.191 (SAC), while the gaps to be explained run
to 0.694.

### The proof: no time-of-use tariff can do it

Enumerating tariffs can only say the ones tried fail. The question is decidable. With an
hour-of-day price that repeats daily, the cost ratio of method *m* to the baseline is

    ratio(p) = sum_h p_h A^m_h / sum_h p_h B_h

with `A^m_h` and `B_h` the method's and baseline's billed imports summed over every
occurrence of hour-of-day *h*. Reproducing published ratio `r_m` is one linear equality,
`sum_h p_h (A^m_h - r_m B_h) = 0`, homogeneous in *p*. Asking whether one tariff serves all
eight rows is then a linear feasibility problem in 24 non-negative unknowns, and it is
**infeasible**:

| what is asked | result |
|---|---|
| all eight rows, our rule's hourly profile as baseline | infeasible for any non-negative 24-hour tariff |
| all eight rows, baseline rescaled to `idle` strength | infeasible for any non-negative 24-hour tariff |
| the five competitive rows only | feasible, but needs peak/offpeak >= **7.31** and prices 18 of 24 hours at zero |

The tariff in the data has a peak-to-offpeak ratio of 2.571. So even on the five rows the
normalising hypothesis already explains, the only tariffs that would reproduce them exactly
charge nothing for three-quarters of the day and seven times the floor in the rest. On all
eight rows no non-negative tariff exists at all, flat, peaky or absurd.

This holds the baseline's *shape* fixed to our rule's day profile, rescaling only its level
-- a genuinely different rule would have a different shape, and with both the tariff and the
baseline profile free the problem stops being falsifiable. The claim is therefore precise:
**the tariff alone cannot be the cause, on any plausible or implausible tariff, given our
hourly import profiles and a baseline shaped like our rule.**

### Their own table says their effective tariff is nearly flat

A corroborating observation about the published table, costing nothing to check: their cost
column and their consumption column agree to within **0.013 on six of eight rows** (SAC
+0.011, MetaEMS +0.001, MARLISA +0.009, MADCQ -0.009, D-MAPPO -0.013, STEMS -0.013; the
exceptions are MADDPG -0.061 and MPC -0.103). A near-flat effective tariff -- or a cost
essentially proportional to imported energy -- would look exactly like that. Ours disagree
by up to 0.191. This is consistent with H3 being *part* of the picture even though it cannot
carry it: it is evidence about their table, not a tariff we can test against our runs.

## 6. H4, the fourth candidate, named because the three fail

Section 4 of `STEMS_COMPARISON.md` invites a fourth hypothesis if the first three fail. Two
things earn the name, and the second is the one we would test next.

**Export treatment** was measured above and is not it on its own. Crediting exports at the
tariff nearly halves the absolute bill (18,773 -> 8,728 USD for the rule) and does improve
the fit -- flat plus export credit reaches 4 of 8 rows within 0.05, the best of any variant
-- but the implied baseline still spans 8,234 to 14,755 USD (CV 0.204) and SAC, MADDPG and
MetaEMS stay out. It is a reparameterisation of the same five-versus-three split.

**The fourth hypothesis, and the one the evidence actually supports: there is no single
cost-column effect.** The eight rows decompose into three groups, none of which is about the
cost definition:

1. **Four rows need no explanation at all once trained.** MARLISA (+0.000), D-MAPPO (+0.014)
   and MADCQ (+0.019) reproduce at fifty episodes against our own 18,773 USD rule under the
   real tariff and the annual-total ratio; MPC's 0.872 is bracketed by its own forecaster
   (causal 0.979, perfect foresight 0.770), which their paper never specifies. These are the
   four rows the fifteen-episode table made look definitional.
2. **Three rows are an energy gap, not a money gap.** At fifty episodes Single-Agent SAC,
   MetaEMS and MADDPG import 1.388, 1.147 and 0.996 times the rule's electricity against the
   paper's 0.813, 0.835 and 0.856 -- consumption disagreements of +0.575, +0.312 and +0.140.
   No cost definition turns more energy into less money. The project has already attributed
   this to the policies themselves rather than to the comparison, and the measurement is in
   `results/action_profile/summary.json`: the mean saturated-action fraction is 86.6 per
   cent (MADDPG), 92.4 (MetaEMS) and 97.4 (SAC) against 0.03 (D-MAPPO), 2.4 (MARLISA) and
   10.2 (MADCQ) for the three that reproduce -- Spearman rho 0.829 against cost over the six
   rows, p = 0.042 -- and MetaEMS takes 3,200 gradient steps where the others take 437,950.
3. **Our own row is still 0.104 short** at a matched budget (0.896 against 0.792) and 0.025
   short warm-started, which is a result about our method, not about their table.

**Export treatment** is the one remaining definitional candidate and it is not supported
either. Crediting exports at the tariff nearly halves the absolute bill (18,773 -> 8,728 USD
for the rule) and does improve the fifteen-episode fit -- flat plus export credit reaches 4
of 8 rows within 0.05, the best of any variant -- but the implied baseline still spans 8,234
to 14,755 USD (CV 0.204) and the same three arms stay out. It reparameterises the split
rather than removing it, and since three rows reproduce exactly with *no* export credit, a
different export treatment would break them.

So the honest fourth hypothesis is a deflationary one: **the cost column was never a single
anomaly, and the fifteen-episode table was the wrong instrument to read it with.** What
remains to be measured is group 2 -- whether fixing the pre-squash saturation moves SAC,
MADDPG and MetaEMS onto their published *consumption* ratios, which would close the cost
rows as a by-product. Both interventions already tried on SAC failed: against a base
saturation of 0.976 / 0.974 / 0.973 on the three action channels at 29,227 USD, a pre-squash
regulariser gives 0.987 / 0.978 / 0.980 at 30,811 USD and return scaling 0.973 / 0.979 /
0.973 at 30,211 USD (`results/action_profile_reg`, `results/action_profile_scaled`). So this
is an open problem, not a pending chore.

## 7. What is not established

* **H1 and H3 were measured at the fifteen-episode budget only.** Both are structural -- an
  aggregation rule and a price vector do not depend on training -- but the specific numbers

* Comfort is untouched by all of this. The defect recorded in `STEMS_COMPARISON.md`

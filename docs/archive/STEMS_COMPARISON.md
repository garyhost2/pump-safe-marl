# Reproducing STEMS Table I, and two columns that do not measure what they claim

Target: Zhang, Wu, Zinflou and Boulet, *Spatial-Temporal Enhanced Safe Multi-Agent
Coordination for Building Energy Management*, arXiv:2510.14112v1.

This note records what reproduces, what does not, and why. Every measured number comes
from `results/t1_repro_ep15/` and is regenerated by

    python -m experiments.diagnostics.stems_table_compare --results results/t1_repro_ep15

## 1. Setup, and how closely it matches

Same scenario: CityLearn, Travis County, eight buildings, 8760 hourly steps, evaluated
in-sample over the same year the policy trained on, which is what their twelve-month
table reports. Same training budget: fifteen episodes, matching their "the model
converges within 15 epochs". Same metric definitions, implemented in `stems/stems_table.py`
from Section III-A item 3 of the paper, including the fixed 20-26 degC comfort band with
no occupancy mask and no set point. Same normalisation: the five ratio columns are
divided by a rule-based baseline, and -- as their 35.1 per cent violation rate implies --
that baseline is an *unshielded* rule, so ours is `rbc` and not `rbc+calibrated`.

One deliberate difference. STEMS impose no district import cap; their constraint set is
state of charge, comfort and equipment. To match it the caps here are set non-binding
(2000 kW district, 500 kW per building). That choice has a consequence recorded in
section 6 rather than hidden: in this codebase the caps also normalise the reward.

## 2. The baseline reproduces

Our unshielded rule violates the state-of-charge and equipment union **31.6 per cent** of
the time against their **35.1**. On a metric neither implementation tuned for, on the
same stock, that is close enough to believe the same baseline is running in both places.

## 4. What does not reproduce at *this budget*: the cost column

> **CORRECTED 2026-10-14 — read `docs/COST_COLUMN_FINDINGS.md` before using this section.**
> The eight-for-eight offset below is real at fifteen episodes and is an **artefact of that
> budget**. At fifty episodes, with no change to the cost definition, tariff, aggregation or
> normaliser, three of the eight rows land on their published figures against this
> repository's own unshielded rule at 18,773 USD: MARLISA 0.847 against 0.847, D-MAPPO 0.817
> against 0.803, MADCQ 0.833 against 0.814, five seeds each. Five rows remain above
> published, and none of those five is a cost-definition gap — one is a forecaster choice
> (MPC, bracketed by our causal 0.979 and oracle 0.770), three are *energy* gaps (SAC,
> MetaEMS, MADDPG, consumption 1.388/1.147/0.996 against published 0.813/0.835/0.856), and
> one is our own arm. All three hypotheses this section's closing paragraph names were
> tested and all three fail: aggregation moves rows by at most 0.0079, the normalising rule
> is refuted because its implied baseline moves with training budget, and the tariff is
> excluded by a linear-feasibility proof. The sentence "the offset is not explained by
> training budget alone" at the end of this section is therefore **wrong**: budget explains
> three of the eight rows completely.

Every one of the eight published cost figures sits below what we measure for the same

In absolute terms the rule costs 18,773 and doing nothing costs 22,981, so our SAC
(28,497) and MetaEMS (24,682) are worse than switching the controller off.

A single weak implementation would show up in one row. Eight for eight, including
methods neither group invented, points at the cost definition, the tariff, or the
strength of the normalising rule. We do not claim to know which, and say so. What we can
say is that the offset is not explained by training budget alone: at fifty episodes our
own arm reaches 0.896, and 0.817 warm-started, which closes most but not all of it.

## 5. Two columns that do not measure what they claim

### 5.1 Discomfort measures the weather file and the building sample

On the unpatched simulator the discomfort proportion is not a function of the controller
at all. CityLearn's indoor temperature observation reads the uncontrolled dataset series,
so the metric reduces to a statistic of the input data. Measured, not argued: an
unpatched rollout reports **0.429329832** and the raw CSVs over the same horizon give
**0.429329832** -- the same 30084/70072, difference 0.00e+00. Patched, the same rule
reports 0.445513186, so the defect *flatters* comfort.

Whatever survives that is dominated by which buildings were drawn. Across the hundred
building Travis County stock the per-building rate runs from **0.000 to 0.856**. The best
eight average 0.010 and the worst eight 0.732; a random eight-building subset has a 5-95
range of **[0.137, 0.381]**. Their 0.130 sits at the **3.9th percentile** of that
distribution and our subset's 0.429 above the 95th. Both are atypical draws from one
stock. Reproduce with `experiments/diagnostics/comfort_is_the_dataset.py`.

Their own table agrees. Rule-Based reports 0.130 and STEMS 0.132, so the rule is the most
comfortable controller in Table I -- more comfortable than every learned method in it.

### 5.2 The safety column contradicts its own definition

Section III-A item 3 defines the safety violation rate as a union of events including the
thermal comfort bound. The STEMS row reports discomfort **0.132** and safety **0.056**.
A union containing an event of probability 0.132 cannot have probability 0.056.

Our own numbers make the arithmetic concrete: the MPC row here reports a
comfort-inclusive union of 44.55 per cent, exactly 100 times its discomfort proportion of
0.4455, because its state-of-charge and equipment terms are both zero. When nothing else
fires, the union *is* the comfort term. `tests/test_stems_table.py` pins the inequality.

## 6. Caveats in our own setup

* **The caps do double duty.** They are the constraint threshold and the reward's
  normaliser, via `(grid_draw / P_grid_max)**2` and `|e_i| / P_building_max`. Loosening
  them to match the STEMS constraint set also changed the objective: measured on a real
  rollout, the three stability terms fall from 21 per cent of the objective under binding
  caps (33.5/11.5) to **0.27 per cent** under 2000/500. In the reproduction runs the
  objective is 58 per cent cost and 42 per cent comfort and essentially nothing else.
* **Comfort is bought at the meter.** Our discomfort is 0.405 against the rule's 0.446
  while cost is 1.011. Every learned method in their table goes the other way. We are on
  the same cost-comfort frontier, on the opposite side of it, and neither paper plots the
  frontier.
* **Our SAC is weak** -- it was also 91 per cent above the oracle in the architecture
  grid. We do not use it to argue that their SAC number is wrong.
* **Fifteen episodes is their budget, not a converged one.** `results/learning_budget`
  shows cost still falling out to roughly fifty episodes while every other term has
  settled. A 50-episode run is in flight.

## 7. Where we are ahead at their own budget

Peak 0.710 against 0.821, ramping 0.721 against 0.883, and the state-of-charge and
equipment union at 0.000 against 5.600. The first two were obtained while those reward
terms were 0.27 per cent of the objective -- they are by-products of cost-aware
arbitrage, not the result of optimising for them.

## 8. Correction: how many baselines the 50-episode result beats

An earlier commit message and session note claimed the warm-started 50-episode cost of
0.817 was below five of the eight published baselines. It is below **four**: MPC 0.872,
MARLISA 0.847, MetaEMS 0.836 and Single-Agent SAC 0.824. It remains above MADCQ 0.814,
D-MAPPO 0.803, MADDPG 0.795 and STEMS 0.792. The four names listed alongside the word
"five" were always the right four; the count was wrong.

The 50-episode numbers themselves are unchanged: cost 1.011 at fifteen episodes, 0.896
at fifty, 0.817 at fifty with the behaviour-cloning warm start, against 0.792 published
for STEMS. Peak 0.642 and ramping 0.675 against their 0.821 and 0.883, and the
state-of-charge and equipment union at 0.000 against their 0.056.

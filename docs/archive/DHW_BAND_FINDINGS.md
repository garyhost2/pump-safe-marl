# The hot-water comfort band, the disinfection phase, and the recourse deadline

*Branch `dhw-band`, from `f5d5744`. Implements `docs/GUARANTEES_MULTINODE.md` claim 2 (M4)
and the M6 fallback on the power-limited plant `stems/dhw_plant.py`. Every number below
was produced by a script in this branch and is read from its JSON output; §9 lists the
commands. The appendix tables are generated by `experiments/diagnostics/dhw_band_tables.py`
from those JSONs, not typed. **Every Thermia parameter is an unsourced fixture**; so are
the temperatures (inlet 10, rated 60, band 45–55, disinfection 60 °C), the conduction
coefficient 0.1 and the bottom-node ceiling. The source temperature is the outdoor
dry-bulb series, standing in for a ground loop that is not modelled.*

## 1. Premises in the brief, checked

1. **"`stems/cbf.py` safe_interval silently mid-points."** The mid-pointing is in
   `stems/battery.py` (`BatteryModel.safe_interval`); `CBFShield.project` calls it. It now
   records every crossing (`last_crossed`, `crossed_total`; `CBFShield.interval_crossings`)
   and takes `on_crossed="midpoint"|"floor"|"ceiling"`, default `"midpoint"`, so the old
   call is unchanged. **What the flag changes, measured: nothing on these devices** — 0 of
   128 064 evaluations cross, against both the enforced bounds and 0.1–0.9
   (`battery_crossing.json`). Crossing needs `lo > hi` or a band narrower than the
   bisection resolution, neither of which the shipped bounds produce.
2. **Unmet hot water "only at 0.25×".** True on the 336-step window the premise was
   measured on. Over the full year, with the rule applied to the year's mean daily demand
   (17–24 % below the first fortnight's for seven houses, 19.5 % above it for the eighth), unmet hot water is non-zero at
   0.5× on `ShadowTank` (4.34 kWh) and, on `DHWPlant` with one node and the thermostat, at
   every size: 136.01 / 6.96 / 0.81 / 0.63 kWh at 0.25× / 0.5× / 1× / 2×, and 0.63 kWh
   with the 12 kW fixture. The last two are equal because `DHWPlant` serves the draw from
   the store *before* charging: a draw larger than the stored energy goes unmet whatever
   the source, so that residue is store-limited, not source-limited.
3. **The just-in-time numbers** (1/16 at 1× with a median reserve, 10/16 at q0.9, 0/16
   at 2×) are **reproduced exactly** by the re-run with the default trigger (§5), and the
   full year confirms the non-monotonicity on 26× the sample.
4. **Two-node domination.** M5's attack 1 (bottom injection) does not apply to the
   committed plant's default, which charges the **top** node. What the committed plant
   does instead is draw energy **top node first** and never heat the bottom except by
   conduction, so with κ = 0 the bottom half is dead storage: 0 windows with the bottom at
   60 °C, per-window bottom peaks of 10.3–13.0 °C, and unmet hot water of 216.8 kWh even
   at 2× (vs 0.63 kWh with one node). M5.5's conclusion — enforcing the comfort node
   certifies nothing about hygiene — is therefore confirmed on the committed plant by
   measurement, not only by argument.

## 2. Defects found in the committed plant (not fixed; reported)

* **The disinfection level is unreachable post-step.** `DHWPlant.step` applies the
  standing loss after the charge, so no post-step node exceeds `1 − loss` — 0.9917–0.9976
  on the eight reference tanks (59.58–59.88 °C). With 60 °C = soc 1.00 the cycle could never
  complete if completion were tested on the post-step state. Completion is therefore
  counted when **the charged node is filled to rated capacity within the step**
  (post-charge, before conduction and loss) — a declared convention, not a sourced rule.
* **`DHWPlant.max_charge_thermal_kwh` reads the pre-draw sink**, while the step charges at
  the post-draw sink. Over a year with the thermostat, the two differ on 1 208 (0.25×),
  1 792 (1×) and 7 977 (1–12 kW fixture) of 70 072 building-steps, by up to 0.237 / 0.237
  / 2.569 kWh, and the applied limit is the **higher** one every time (the draw cools the
  sink). A ceiling built on the pre-draw limit would under-predict heating and overshoot;
  `DHWStoreModel.charge_limit_kwh` is the applied one (`exactness.json`).
* **Conduction does not conserve energy when `top_fraction != 0.5`.** The shift is in soc
  units and is multiplied by each node's own capacity: an 8 kWh tank, top full, bottom
  empty, κ = 0.2, gains +0.16 kWh in one step at `top_fraction = 0.4` and +0.32 kWh at
  0.3. Exact at 0.5, which every run here uses. Fix suggested, not applied: move
  `shift · C_top · C_bot / (C_top + C_bot) · 2` from one node to the other.
* **Top-first draws can invert the profile**, contrary to the module docstring's "the
  inversion does not arise". Rare in practice: 23 of 70 072 building-steps end with the top node colder than the
  bottom (two nodes, κ = 0, thermostat, 1×); 296 draws exceeded the top node's energy.

## 3. Item 1 — the exact one-step inverse (`stems/dhw_barrier.py: DHWStoreModel`)

`DHWStoreModel` is `DHWPlant.step` written as a pure function vectorised over buildings,
with the same floating-point operations in the same order: serve the draw top-first,
evaluate the compressor capacity at the **post-draw** sink temperature, cap by the
post-draw headroom, conduct, clip, apply loss. The action is `u ∈ [0, 1]` with
`request = u · (power_max + element) · dt`. Intervals are found by bisection on the model
(64 iterations, stopping at adjacent doubles); the returned endpoint is always a point
where the constraint was *evaluated and held*, so feasibility of the endpoint does not
depend on monotonicity — only minimality does.

Measured (`exactness.json`, seed 0): 20 configurations (1/2 nodes, top/bottom charge,
κ ∈ {0, 0.2}, element 0/1.5 kW, element allowed or not, `top_fraction` 0.4/0.5),
48 000 building-steps, **max forward error 0.0 kWh**; 25 934 binding endpoints checked by
stepping a fresh `DHWPlant` at the returned action, **max |soc − bound| = 3.33e-16, 0 on
the wrong side**. Every node's successor is non-decreasing in `u` (tested on a 201-point
grid), which is what makes each node's admissible set an interval.
`safe_interval` has `BatteryModel`'s signature, so `FlexibleLoad.has_band` is true for a
tank for the first time; its float32 output is rounded inward so a float32 action array
cannot leave the interval (tested).

## 4. Items 2 and 3 — the intersection and the cycle phase

**Intersection.** `project` intersects the node intervals and, when the intersection is
empty, returns a declared fallback (`floor_first`: largest node floor; `ceiling_first`:
smallest node ceiling) and a per-building record: `status` per node
(`FLOOR_UNREACHABLE` / `CEILING_UNAVOIDABLE`), `conflict` (each node feasible alone, not
jointly), and the predicted breach the fallback accepts. A constructed two-node case
(κ = 0.5, top ceiling 0.70, bottom floor 0.38) exercises the conflict path in both
fallbacks. In the diagnostic, **every** empty intersection was `floor_unreachable`;
`ceiling_unavoidable` and `conflict` never occurred in 60 shielded year runs
(4 204 320 building-steps). With top charging the bottom can gain heat only from the top,
so after a guarded exit its ceiling is implied by the top's — the two-node intersection is
implemented and tested, but on this plant it never binds on the bottom node.

**Cycle phase.** `CyclePhaseBarrier` carries `z = (node soc, q, k)`. Bands on the top node
are [0.70, 0.90] in N and [0.70, 1.00] in D; the bottom node gets the same ceilings and no
floor (chosen). A transition is admitted only if every banded node is inside its band in
**both** phases; otherwise it is refused, `q` is unchanged, and the refusal is logged with
its reason. The band of the phase in force has the final word over the deadline, so the
deadline is clipped in N (it requests D first; clipping happened on 0–5 steps per run).

What the guard costs, at 1× (one node, thermostat, recourse): 2 037 refusals in a year
(2 020 above the ceiling, 17 below the floor); the relaxed ceiling after a completed cycle
lasted **5.72 h per cycle** (2 035 h over 356 cycles), 5.7–8.8 h across sizes — well under
M4.4's no-draw bound, which on these eight tanks (loss 0.00237–0.00833) is 13–45 h, because
draws pull the tank down. What it buys: the unshielded price-aware policy, which leaves D
as soon as its cycle completes, spends 2 964 building-steps above 55 °C (max 4.76 °C over);
shielded, **0**. The strict guard (floor included, as the brief specifies) also refuses
transitions from states below the floor — 482 times at 0.25× for the thermostat; §6.5
measures the ceiling-only variant.

## 5. Item 4 — the recourse deadline (`LegionellaCycleBarrier(trigger=...)`)

The just-in-time trigger forces at `ceil(gap / net_rate)` steps before the deadline with a
**per-step** reserve deducted from the rate. The recourse trigger re-plans every step:
it rolls the tank's own dynamics forward at full output against a causal hour-of-day draw
forecast (`HourOfDayDrawForecast`) with a **lump** safety stock drawn at the start of the
plan, and forces when the window left is no longer than the plan; the forced action is
the smallest command completing this step against forecast plus stock. `trigger="jit"`
stays the default everywhere.

`experiments/diagnostics/legionella_recourse.py` re-runs the legionella_demo plant,
thermostat and draws on sizing {0.25, 0.5, 1, 2}× × reserve quantile {0.5, 0.75, 0.9,
0.95, 1.0} × trigger. The quantiles are over the whole run, as legionella_demo takes them
(a look-ahead in the reserve *value*, for both triggers; kept for comparability).

**Success criterion: completion monotone in source size — met by recourse, measured as
missed windows.** At every one of the five reserves, on both 336 h and 8 759 h, a larger
source never misses more windows under recourse (year, q0.9: 185 / 128 / 51 / 28). The
just-in-time trigger is non-monotone at q0.5 (year: 416 / 409 / 354 / 378) and q0.75
(250 / 295 / 187 / 177), and `LegionellaSpec` refuses it outright at q ≥ 0.9 for the
small sources (reserve ≥ source output). Tables in the appendix.

**The completion *count* is not monotone, and should not be used as the criterion.** A
store held at the level "completes" every hour it stays there, and where forcing is
continuous a store that dips and recovers completes again: raw counts reach 2 171 per year
for the just-in-time trigger at 0.25× q0.75 (91 in 336 h), and recourse at 0.25× completes
more events than at 0.5× (q0.5: 407 vs 185) for the same reason. Missed windows — a week
with no disinfection — are the hygiene failure; they are what is monotone.

**What recourse costs.** More electricity, because it starts earlier and holds the store
hotter for longer. Year, 1×: no cycle 8 175.8 kWh; just-in-time q0.9 9 448.8; recourse q0.9
9 566.2 (+1.2 % over just-in-time, with 51 instead of 94 missed windows). At q1.0 recourse
misses 2 windows at 1× and 0 at 2×, for 9 670.5 and 10 874.2 kWh.

## 6. Item 5 — the diagnostic on the power-limited plant

`experiments/diagnostics/dhw_band_diagnostic.py`: 8 759 h × 8 houses, five unit families
(sizing-rule fixtures at 0.25/0.5/1/2× of mean daily demand / 4 h, and the 1–12 kW
fixture), three tank configurations (one node; two nodes with κ = 0 and κ = 0.1), two
policies (`thermostat` at 50 °C; `price_aware`: 54 °C in the 0.21–0.22 tariff hours,
45.5 °C in the 17–21 h peak, disinfection requested after 120 h of the window and heated
only in cheap hours), each unshielded and shielded with either trigger (reserve and
safety stock = each house's q0.9 hourly draw). 90 runs, full tables in the appendix.

### 6.1 Band and refusals
* Shielded, the only band violations are the reported empty intersections, all on the
  floor. At 1× (one node, thermostat) 1 355 building-steps (1.9 %) cannot be held at 45 °C
  by any action; at 0.25×, 13 423 (19.2 %); with the 1–12 kW fixture, 0.
* Claim 2's floor condition, evaluated on the data from the worst in-band state, fails on
  10 257 / 6 821 / 2 470 / 695 building-steps at 0.25× / 0.5× / 1× / 2× (one node), and on
  0 for the 1–12 kW fixture: **the floor of the band is guaranteed only by a source sized
  like the 12 kW fixture**, the same conclusion M4.4 drew from CityLearn's heater.

### 6.2 Energy, cost, starts
* The shield adds the weekly cycle for little energy: 1×, one node, thermostat, 3 447.1 →
  3 489.3 kWh (+1.2 %), cost 979.18 → 995.59 (+1.7 %), with 356 cycles instead of none.
* The price-aware policy, shielded, cuts cost by 8.6 % against the shielded thermostat
  (909.85 vs 995.59) while using 13.1 % more electricity (3 946.2 vs 3 489.3 kWh).
* Compressor starts rise with unit size because more requests fall below the modulation
  floor: 4 485 starts per house-year at 0.25× to 8 200 with the 1–12 kW fixture (a start
  in 93.6 % of hours; the count is at most one per hour, so it is a lower bound).
* The just-in-time trigger with a q0.9 reserve **degenerates on this plant** at 0.25× and
  0.5×: the reserve exceeds the source, the rate floors, and it forces permanently (D phase
  on 53.8–58.5 k of 70.1 k building-steps), using 2.2× and 3.6× the electricity of recourse. The
  plant charges for heat it spills, so this is partly an accounting artefact of `DHWPlant`.

### 6.3 Negative result: the guarantee needs the step's draw
With the barrier given the causal forecast of the step's draw instead of the draw itself
(`--barrier-draw forecast`), the exactness is for the wrong draw. With the 1–12 kW fixture
(one node, thermostat, recourse) violations go from 0 to 657 a year (617 floor, 40 ceiling,
up to 4.88 °C above 55 °C) and only 26 steps were reported empty — **at least 631
violations are silent**; missed windows go from 0 to 105. Across the 24 shielded forecast
runs, violations minus reported empties (a lower bound on the silent ones) is 631–2 698
building-steps a year. The guarantee is conditional on
knowing the step's draw, which CityLearn's observation provides and a real controller
does not.

### 6.4 Recourse vs just-in-time on `DHWPlant`
Missed windows, thermostat, 0.25× / 0.5× / 1× / 2× / 1–12 kW:

| tank | recourse | just-in-time |
|---|---|---|
| one node | 126 / 102 / 60 / 24 / 0 | 31 / 24 / 84 / 51 / 72 |
| two nodes, κ = 0 | 84 / 73 / 34 / 10 / 0 | 5 / 5 / 22 / 73 / 120 |
| two nodes, κ = 0.1 | 85 / 78 / 42 / 11 / 0 | 14 / 12 / 30 / 74 / 118 |

Recourse is monotone in all six policy × tank series (price-aware: 28/0/0/0/0,
3/0/0/0/0, 13/0/0/0/0). Just-in-time misses **more** windows with a bigger source above
0.5×; its low counts at 0.25×/0.5× are the permanent-forcing degeneration of §6.2.

### 6.5 The ceiling-only guard
M4.3's second form, which does not check the floor at a transition, run at 0.25×, 0.5×
and 1× (one node, both policies, both triggers; 12 shielded runs): missed windows, band
violations and empty intersections are **identical** to the strict guard's in all 12 runs,
and electricity agrees to 0.1 kWh. The only difference is the 9–1 500 below-floor
refusals per run, which disappear. The strict guard's floor check therefore costs nothing
measurable here: a refused entry below the floor delays the transition while the deadline
keeps heating, and the tank is back above 45 °C within the window.

## 7. What was not done, or done differently

* `charge_node="bottom"` is supported and tested for exactness but was **not run** in the
  diagnostic (it needs `allow_inversion=True`, i.e. unmodelled buoyancy).
* No buoyancy or plug-flow draw was added to `DHWPlant`; the defects in §2 are reported,
  not fixed, because every number here is defined on the committed plant.
* The draw is known to the barrier in the main diagnostic (as in legionella_demo); §6.3
  is the only measurement without that assumption.
* The two-node conflict path never occurs on this plant; it is covered by one constructed
  test only.
* `CyclePhaseBarrier`'s just-in-time trigger floors its rate rather than refusing a reserve
  at or above the source output, unlike `LegionellaSpec`; §6.2 shows the consequence.
* Merging this branch changes `stems/*.py`, and therefore `meta.code.fingerprint`: a
  resumable grid run after the merge will not skip records made before it.
